# Check Desk Step 1 — niche scope confirmation

**Date:** 2026-11-10 (America/Denver).  
**Pilot market:** Denver / Denver metro (confirmed).  
**Status:** Planning only. This file confirms the niche; it does not collect listings or recommend products.

## What Check Desk is

A factual, verified local directory of places where someone can cash a check, buy or cash a money order, pay a bill in person, or reload a prepaid card — written for people who may not have a bank account, who need service after typical bank hours, or who want a lower-fee option than a storefront that does not publish its prices.

It is an information site, not a financial institution and not a financial adviser.

## Services covered (in scope)

1. **Check cashing** — payroll, government, tax-refund, insurance, business, money-order, and (only where an official page says so) personal or two-party checks. Record check types, limits, ID rules, and fees only as official pages state them.
2. **Money orders** — buying and, where official pages say it is offered, cashing. USPS, Walmart, Western Union and MoneyGram agents, grocery money desks, and other official sellers.
3. **Bill pay** — in-person walk-up bill payment at money-service desks or similar counters (utilities, rent, phones, etc.), including payment-method and stub rules when official pages publish them.
4. **Prepaid reload** — adding cash or a cashed check to a prepaid debit card at a public retail desk, plus CFPB-style education about fee *types*. This is not a ranked list of "best cards" and not an endorsement.

Related context that may appear in guides, with sources, but is not a fifth listing category: cheaper alternatives that official pages actually describe (for example, cashing a USPS money order free at a Post Office). Do not invent a legal duty for banks to cash checks for non-customers unless a cited official rule says so — public guides disagree on Regulation CC (see the competitor memo).

## Audience

- **Unbanked and underbanked households.** The FDIC's 2023 Household Survey (official page last updated November 14, 2024; opened 2026-11-10: https://www.fdic.gov/household-survey) reports that 4.2% of U.S. households (about 5.6 million) were unbanked and 14.2% (about 19.0 million) were underbanked, and that unbanked households use nonbank money orders, check cashing, and money transfers for core transactions such as paying bills and receiving income more often than banked households do.
- **After-hours users.** People who need a desk open outside Monday–Friday 09:00–17:00 America/Denver, including evenings and weekends.
- **People comparing fees honestly**, including users who *do* have a bank but need a money order, same-day cash, or a Sunday location.

Write in plain language. Do not assume the reader has an account, a smartphone-only workflow, or a credit file.

## Out of scope (do not promote or treat as a Check Desk service)

- **Payday loans, title loans, pawn loans, cash advances, buy-now-pay-later, and similar credit products.** We do not promote them, rank them, or write "best payday loan" copy. Whether a storefront that mainly makes those loans may still appear as a *check-cashing location* is an open question for Richard (see PLAN.md).
- **Financial, legal, or tax advice.** No recommendations to open or close an account, take a loan, or choose a card. "Favor lower-fee options" means honest, sourced comparisons, not advice to use a particular product.
- **Business outreach.** No calling, emailing, or visiting stores to gather fees. No mystery shopping.
- **Affiliate programs, paid placements, paid rankings, and spending project money** to acquire data or traffic.
- **Wire/remittance shopping as a primary product** (Western Union/MoneyGram *money orders* and in-store bill pay are in scope; international remittance rate shopping is not the v1 directory).
- **Bank account opening, second-chance checking product rankings, and credit-repair services.** A short, sourced pointer that accounts exist is enough if a later guide needs it; it is not a product marketplace.
- **Invented fee averages** ("stores typically charge 1.5%–5%") copied from other publishers.

## Verification standard (applies to every in-scope fact)

Every data point needs a public source URL and a last-verified date. If an official page does not publish a national or store fee, say "no national price published" (or the page's own wording) instead of estimating. Label single-store examples as examples. Recheck before changing a figure.

## Relationship to Errand Desk

Sibling project, not a section of a city errand page. Errand Desk already has a sourced money-order guide and Denver document-errand pages. Check Desk may reuse method, templates, and re-checked official money-order facts, but it has a different audience, stricter no-affiliate rule for this project, and a location-level directory that Errand Desk does not attempt.
