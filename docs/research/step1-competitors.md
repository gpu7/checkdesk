# Check Desk Step 1 — public competitor scan

**Date:** 2026-11-10 (America/Denver).  
**Method:** WebSearch to find candidates, then WebFetch (and, where a fetch timed out, a direct read of the official public HTML) of each page cited below.  
**Rule:** Only pages actually opened are described as reviewed. Search hits that were not opened are listed at the end as unreviewed leads. No listings were collected for Check Desk. Fee figures mentioned here are what *those sites* display; they are not Check Desk facts unless the same figure was also read on an official provider page (see PLAN.md).

## Reviewed sites

### 1. SecondChanceInfo — national + Denver directory (closest direct competitor)

- **URLs opened:** https://secondchanceinfo.com/check-cashing and https://secondchanceinfo.com/check-cashing/denver-co
- **What it covers:** A 98-metro check-cashing directory (the national page claims "3,134+" locations). City pages list chain and independent storefronts with addresses, phones, and some hours, plus a "cheaper ways" narrative (own bank, issuing bank, Walmart, grocery desks, apps). Denver is one of the metros (~40 locations claimed on the city page, last updated 2026-08-14).
- **Freshness / verification:** National and Denver pages both say "Last updated: 2026-08-14" (about three months before this scan). Sources on the national page include chain sites, a Federal Reserve Regulation CC cite, the FDIC Household Survey, FTC scam guidance, and "OpenStreetMap money services data (ODbL)." Individual Denver rows are not each stamped with a last-checked date or a per-row source URL.
- **Fee transparency:** A comparison table of "typical" fees ("Published fee schedules and typical ranges, June 2026") rather than quoted official wording on every cell. Walk-in stores are summarized as "1.5–5%." Walmart is stated as a flat $4 / $8. Denver ACE rows do not show a store-level fee.
- **Ads / affiliate model:** The opened pages did not present an on-page ad unit or a clear "(paid link)" disclosure in the fetched text. The site also promotes its own second-chance checking and prepaid guides. Monetization model is not fully documented on the pages opened.
- **Weaknesses:** OSM-backed "local" lists on the Denver page include businesses that are not obviously check cashers (for example, bail-bond and mortgage names appear in the fetched list), and some ACE stores appear twice. The embedded map is described as "an independent Google search of the area — not a map of the listings on this page." The page asserts that Regulation CC requires a bank to cash a check drawn on itself for non-customers; NerdWallet's opened guide (below) says banks and credit unions are *not* required to cash checks for non-customers. Check Desk must not repeat either legal claim until an official rule text is opened and quoted. Hours and after-hours coverage are incomplete (the Denver page itself says some listings lack hours). No money-order or bill-pay directory comparable to the check-cashing list.

### 2. MoneyPantry — grocery-store fee guide

- **URL opened:** https://moneypantry.com/grocery-stores-that-cash-checks/
- **What it covers:** A 2026 chain-by-chain guide titled "45 Grocery Stores That Cash Checks," with a top-five table (Walmart, Kroger, Wegmans, Safeway/Albertsons, WinCo) and per-banner notes on fees, limits, and check types. King Soopers is included as the Colorado/Wyoming Kroger banner. Also lists some chains the author says do *not* cash checks.
- **Freshness / verification:** "Last Updated & Fact-Checked: January 8, 2026" and a "fact-checked against primary sources" badge. An August 29, 2025 update note (Ralphs in Ventura County) shows some reactive corrections. It is a national chain guide, not a Denver store directory.
- **Fee transparency:** Many fee figures are hedged ("around," "typically," "varies by location") and are not accompanied by a visible official URL next to each number in the fetched text. That is weaker than an Errand Desk-style numbered source list.
- **Ads / affiliate model:** The byline states "Some links may earn me a small commission" / "Contains affiliate links." The fetched page also included an unrelated product promotion (HealthyWage) in the body.
- **Weaknesses:** Commenters on the same page said some listed stores did not cash checks when they tried (Giant Eagle, Ralphs in Ventura County). The author added caveats, which is honest, but it shows chain-level guides go stale store-by-store. No after-hours Denver finder, no money-order/bill-pay location list, and no last-checked date on each fee cell.

### 3. Bankrate — "how to cash a check without a bank account"

- **URL opened:** https://www.bankrate.com/banking/checking/how-to-cash-a-check-without-a-bank-account/
- **What it covers:** A national explainer ("6 Ways…"), published August 31, 2026, with a fee comparison table (own account, issuing bank, Walmart or Kroger, apps, prepaid cards, signing over a check, check-cashing stores). Mentions FDIC unbanked figures and names ACE Cash Express, Check Into Cash, and Amscot as storefront examples.
- **Freshness / verification:** Dated August 31, 2026, with a named writer and a "reviewed by" line. Individual table cells are not linked to official provider URLs in the fetched text.
- **Fee transparency:** Mix of specific Walmart figures and wide ranges (check-cashing stores "1% to 10%"; issuing banks "$6 to $8" with some bank names). Useful as a shopper overview, not as a citable fee schedule.
- **Ads / affiliate model:** The page states it may contain references to partner products and links to "how we make money." Editorial independence is claimed; it still ends by pushing readers to open a checking account (Bankrate's broader model is financial-product marketplace).
- **Weaknesses:** No local directory, no hours, no last-verified stamps on each figure, and a strong "avoid check-cashing stores / open an account" frame that is advice-like. Not built for after-hours or bill-pay trips.

### 4. NerdWallet — "How to Cash a Check (And Save on Fees)"

- **URL opened:** https://www.nerdwallet.com/banking/learn/cash-check-paying-high-fees
- **What it covers:** A general how-to, updated April 23, 2026, on cashing or depositing a check at your own bank, the payer's bank, or a major retailer, plus prepaid-card load fees as a concept. Warns that payday lending stores are an expensive option. Recommends a checking account, including second-chance accounts, as the long-term path.
- **Freshness / verification:** "Updated Apr 23, 2026," with a writer bio and an expert-verification blurb. Older than the Bankrate piece and still more general.
- **Fee transparency:** Mostly qualitative ("generally for less than $10 per check"; prepaid cards "usually a monthly service fee of about $5"). Less specific on Walmart's official maximums than Bankrate or MoneyPantry (those figures appear in other NerdWallet URLs that came up in search but were not opened for this memo).
- **Ads / affiliate model:** Footer and disclosures describe compensation relationships (for example, investment-account referrals). Classic marketplace/affiliate media site.
- **Weaknesses:** No city directory, no after-hours tool, no bill-pay or money-order comparison, and a banking-product funnel. Helpful context, not a substitute for a verified local list.

### 5. Official chain pages and locators (what users already have)

These are not "directories" in the editorial sense, but they are the pages people — and Check Desk — should use as primary sources. Opened for this step:

- **Walmart check cashing** — https://www.walmart.com/cp/check-cashing/632047 (official HTML opened 2026-11-10). Publishes national *maximum* fees and limits in an FAQ (see PLAN.md for the wording). No store list or Denver hours on this page. Points customers to a Money Service Center or Customer Service desk.
- **Walmart money orders** — https://www.walmart.com/cp/money-orders/5791922 (opened 2026-11-10). Publishes a $1 maximum fee; exact charges "vary by location, but they won't be over $1." Cash or debit at a Money Center or Customer Service Desk.
- **King Soopers / Kroger Money Services** — https://www.kingsoopers.com/o/money-services/check-cashing , https://www.kingsoopers.com/o/money-services/bill-pay , and https://www.kroger.com/i/money-services-faq (all opened 2026-11-10). Colorado-relevant grocery money desk: check cashing, bill pay, money orders, prepaid. **No dollar check-cashing or bill-pay fee on these pages** ("Fees and limits vary by state"). Check limit "up to $5,000." ID + SSN/TIN list. Generally "8am-8pm," many stores seven days, "hours may vary." Store locator is linked from the pages. Bill-pay examples include many non-Colorado billers; do not treat those examples as a Denver biller list.
- **ACE Cash Express** — https://www.acecashexpress.com/check-cashing/ (opened 2026-11-10). Services and ID rules; **no national fee** ("menu board," "vary by state"). Official locator: https://www.acecashexpress.com/locations/ . The broader ACE site also markets payday and title loans, which Check Desk will not promote.
- **USPS money orders** — https://www.usps.com/shop/money-orders.htm (opened 2026-11-10). Clear national fee table, cashing rules, and replacement fee. Location-level hours are not on this page.
- **Western Union money orders** — https://www.westernunion.com/us/en/money-order.html (opened 2026-11-10). Fee "varying by location"; location finder referenced; not every agent cashes money orders.
- **MoneyGram money-order limits** — https://www.moneygram.com/blog/money-order-limits-what-you-need-to-know (opened 2026-11-10; article dated July 1, 2026). US limit $1,000; no national fee; location finder referenced.

**Weaknesses of official locators as a user-facing substitute:** each brand is a silo; fees are often "ask the store"; payday products are mixed into some chain sites; there is no cross-brand after-hours or lower-fee comparison; no independent last-verified stamp.

### Official consumer-education pages (not directories)

- **CFPB — prepaid card fee types:** https://www.consumerfinance.gov/ask-cfpb/what-types-of-fees-do-prepaid-cards-typically-charge-en-2053/ (opened 2026-11-10; "last reviewed: OCT 31, 2024"). Excellent fee-*type* explainer (activation, monthly, cash reload, bill payment, etc.). No locator, no Denver content, and the review date is two years old.
- **FDIC 2023 Household Survey:** https://www.fdic.gov/household-survey (opened 2026-11-10; last updated November 14, 2024). Best official source for unbanked/underbanked prevalence and use of nonbank check cashing and money orders. Not a place directory.

## Gaps Check Desk can fill

1. **Sourced, dated facts in the Errand Desk style.** Every fee, hour, and service claim gets a public URL and a last-verified date. "No national price published" instead of "typical 1.5%–5%."
2. **A local directory that is not just check-cashing storefronts.** Combine lower-fee published options (USPS money orders, Walmart published maximums, King Soopers Money Services) with after-hours specialists, plus money orders, bill pay, and prepaid reload — which the reviewed directories either omit or treat in passing.
3. **An after-hours finder with an explicit definition** (outside Mon–Fri 09:00–17:00 America/Denver), built from published hours, not a generic "open now" map widget that may not match the listings.
4. **Cleaner location data than OSM-scraped metro lists.** Official locators first; do not publish bail-bond or payday-loan storefronts as if they were verified money-service desks unless Richard decides they may appear, labeled, because they also cash checks.
5. **Honest lower-fee ordering without product-marketplace advice.** No "open this account" funnel, no payday/title-loan promotion, no affiliate signups, no paid rankings.
6. **A Denver-first, Colorado-aware pilot** that can cite DORA money-transmitter pages carefully (and can research check-casher licensing as a TODO, without claiming a rule the official pages did not state).
7. **Unresolved legal questions left unresolved until official text is read.** In particular, do not copy SecondChanceInfo's Regulation CC "must cash for non-customers" claim or NerdWallet's contrary claim until the official rule is opened.
