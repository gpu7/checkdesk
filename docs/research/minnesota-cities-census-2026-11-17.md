# Minnesota: five largest incorporated places (Census Vintage 2025)

Prepared 2026-11-17 (America/Denver). Same file and method as Alabama, Alaska, Arizona, Arkansas, California, Connecticut, Delaware, Florida, Georgia, Hawaii (CDP exception was one-time only), Idaho, Illinois, Indiana, Iowa, Kansas, Kentucky, Louisiana, Maine, Maryland, Massachusetts, and Michigan. Standing instruction is to research and ship these five, largest first, one PR each, Post Offices only. Minnesota is partitioned into county subdivisions that include many townships (SUMLEV 061). This ranking uses **only SUMLEV 162 incorporated places**. Townships, unorganized territories, and other county-subdivision rows that have no SUMLEV 162 place row are excluded. The order is not ambiguous (see Quirks and the cutoff note below). There are no SUMLEV 170 or 172 consolidated-city rows in this state file, and none of the five is a consolidated government. Hours on the city pages are Central Time for all five (see Time zones below). Grouping is alphabetical: Minnesota after Michigan. Do not start Mississippi from this run.

## Quirks

- **Use SUMLEV 162 incorporated places only, not townships.** Minnesota has 2,673 SUMLEV 061 county-subdivision rows in this file: 846 cities (FUNCSTAT F, coextensive with the incorporated place), 1,775 townships (FUNCSTAT A), and 52 unorganized territories (UT, FUNCSTAT S). Townships and unorganized territories have **no** SUMLEV 162 row in this Census vintage and are excluded from this ranking. The largest such townships are White Bear township (10,399, in Ramsey County) and Big Lake township (8,283, in Sherburne County). All of them are far smaller than Duluth (88,330) and smaller than Brooklyn Park (83,543), so even a combined city-and-township list would have the same top five. Townships without a SUMLEV 162 row stay out of scope. This does not make the ranking ambiguous.
- **Hennepin County has no SUMLEV 061 rows.** Unusually among the four home counties, Hennepin County has zero county-subdivision rows in this file. Its cities, including Minneapolis and Bloomington, appear as SUMLEV 162 places and as SUMLEV 157 county parts with PRIMGEO_FLAG=1, but not as coextensive county subdivisions (no 061 or 071 row). St. Paul, Rochester, and Duluth do have the usual FUNCSTAT F 061 row and FUNCSTAT A 071 row, each equal to the SUMLEV 162 place total. Ranking and city-limits checks still use the SUMLEV 162 place in every case.
- **City and coextensive county subdivision.** Where a 061 row exists, it is a fictitious (FUNCSTAT F) county subdivision coextensive with the city and must not be treated as a second place. Fifty-one SUMLEV 162 cities, including all of Hennepin County’s cities plus a handful in Koochiching, Itasca, Lake of the Woods, and St. Louis Counties, have no same-name 061 row. That file-structure quirk does not change the SUMLEV 162 ranking.
- **Two cities named St. Anthony.** The only duplicated SUMLEV 162 NAME in this state file is “St. Anthony city”: the larger city (PLACE 56680, 9,989) spans Hennepin and Ramsey, and a second city (PLACE 56698, 92) sits in Stearns County. Neither is in this five. Match those rows by PLACE FIPS, not by name alone.
- **No consolidated government in this five, and no multi-county city in this five.** Each of the five has exactly one SUMLEV 157 county part, equal to the SUMLEV 162 total. Multi-county places in this state file, including St. Cloud city (72,647; Benton, Sherburne, and Stearns), sit well below the cutoff and are not shipped in this run.
- **CDPs are out of scope.** The Vintage 2025 incorporated-place file does not include CDPs. The Hawaii CDP exception was one-time only and does not apply to Minnesota.

## Time zones

The central standard time zone under **49 CFR 71.6(a)** “includes that part of the United States that is west of the boundary line between the eastern and central standard time zones described in § 71.5 and east of the boundary line between the central and mountain standard time zones described in § 71.7.” Unlike the Maine clause in § 71.4, § 71.6 does not contain a separate “includes all of the State of Minnesota” sentence, but both bounding lines lie outside Minnesota, so the entire state is in the central zone.

The Eastern–Central boundary in **49 CFR 71.5(a)** is headed “Minnesota-Michigan-Wisconsin” but the described line starts at the junction of the western boundary of Michigan with the United States–Canada boundary and then runs through the western Upper Peninsula of Michigan and down the western boundary of Michigan in Lake Michigan. It does not enter Minnesota. The Central–Mountain boundary in **49 CFR 71.7** starts at the Montana–North Dakota boundary and then runs through North Dakota, South Dakota, Nebraska, Kansas, Oklahoma, and Texas; it also does not enter Minnesota. **49 CFR 71.5(h)** places municipalities located on the eastern–central boundary in the central zone; **49 CFR 71.7(g)** places municipalities on the central–mountain boundary in the mountain zone, except Murdo, South Dakota. None of the five cities sits on either line. Minneapolis (Hennepin County), St. Paul (Ramsey County), Rochester (Olmsted County), Bloomington (Hennepin County), and Duluth (St. Louis County) are all well inside those bounds. Therefore all five are labeled **Central Time**.
