# Michigan: five largest incorporated places (Census Vintage 2025)

Prepared 2026-11-17 (America/Denver). Same file and method as Alabama, Alaska, Arizona, Arkansas, California, Connecticut, Delaware, Florida, Georgia, Hawaii (CDP exception was one-time only), Idaho, Illinois, Indiana, Iowa, Kansas, Kentucky, Louisiana, Maine, Maryland, and Massachusetts. Standing instruction is to research and ship these five, largest first, one PR each, Post Offices only. Michigan is partitioned into county subdivisions that include many townships (SUMLEV 061). This ranking uses **only SUMLEV 162 incorporated places**. Townships, charter townships, and other county-subdivision rows that have no SUMLEV 162 place row are excluded. The order is not ambiguous (see Quirks and the cutoff note below). There are no SUMLEV 170 or 172 consolidated-city rows in this state file, and none of the five is a consolidated government. Hours on the city pages are Eastern Time for all five (see Time zones below). Grouping is alphabetical: Michigan after Massachusetts. Do not start Minnesota from this run.

## Quirks

- **Use SUMLEV 162 incorporated places only, not townships.** Michigan has 1,540 SUMLEV 061 county-subdivision rows in this file: 300 cities (FUNCSTAT F, coextensive with the incorporated place), 1,122 townships, and 118 charter townships. Townships and charter townships have **no** SUMLEV 162 row in this Census vintage and are excluded from this ranking. The largest such townships are Clinton charter township (101,374, in Macomb County), Canton charter township (99,714, in Wayne County), and Macomb township (95,099, in Macomb County), then Shelby charter township (80,415, in Macomb County). All of them are smaller than Ann Arbor (122,233) and smaller than Lansing (113,884), so even a combined city-and-township list would have the same top five. Townships without a SUMLEV 162 row stay out of scope. This does not make the ranking ambiguous.
- **City and coextensive county subdivision.** Each of the five cities is also represented as a county subdivision. For all five places in this ranking, the file carries a SUMLEV 162 “X city” row (FUNCSTAT A), a SUMLEV 061 “X city” row (FUNCSTAT F) with the same population, and a SUMLEV 071 place-part row (FUNCSTAT A) equal to the whole-place total, in one county. Ranking and city-limits checks use the SUMLEV 162 place, not the MCD row. Check Desk pages use the city name (Detroit, not “Detroit city” as a legal-style label in running text except where the Census NAME is quoted). Do not double-count the city and the MCD.
- **Look-alike townships are not the city.** Grand Rapids charter township (Kent County, 19,442) is not Grand Rapids city. Ann Arbor charter township (Washtenaw County, 4,506) is not Ann Arbor city. Warren township (Midland County, 2,045) is not Warren city.
- **Enclaves and nearby municipalities.** Hamtramck city and Highland Park city are enclaves surrounded by Detroit and are separate SUMLEV 162 places; their offices stay off the Detroit list unless a geocode puts a Detroit-printed office inside Detroit city (in which case the usual printed-city versus geocode rules apply). Center Line city is an enclave surrounded by Warren and stays off the Warren list under the same rule. East Grand Rapids city is a separate place from Grand Rapids city.
- **Warren and Sterling Heights are separate places in the same county.** Both are in this five, both in Macomb County. Do not merge the pair, and do not fold other Macomb cities into either list.
- **Only 533 incorporated places, all FUNCSTAT A.** The official Excel table has 533 place rows, matching the CSV: 280 cities and 253 villages. There are no boroughs, towns, or “city (balance)” rows in the SUMLEV 162 universe. “Village of Grosse Pointe Shores city” (2,533) is a city whose legal name still starts with “Village of”; it spans Wayne (2,454) and Macomb (79) and is well outside this five.
- **No SUMLEV 170 or 172 rows.** The Michigan Vintage 2025 state file has none. None of the five is a consolidated city-county government.
- **Some Michigan cities span counties; none of this five does.** Lansing city, just outside the five, has three SUMLEV 157 parts (Ingham 108,771, Eaton 5,113, Clinton 0). Northville city, Milan city, Richmond city, Memphis city, Casnovia village, and Village of Grosse Pointe Shores city also have county parts. Each of the five in this ranking has a single SUMLEV 157 part equal to the whole-place total, in one county, as noted in the table.
- **CDPs are excluded.** This SUB-IP / sub-est file is incorporated places only; no CDP names appear. Those CDPs are out of scope, consistent with earlier states.
- **Whole place, not county parts.** Ranking uses the SUMLEV 162 whole-place total, consistent with earlier states. Every SUMLEV 162 row has COUNTY = 000.

## Time zones

The eastern standard time zone under **49 CFR 71.4** “includes that part of the United States that is west of 67°30″ W. longitude and east of the boundary line described in § 71.5, and includes all of the State of Maine, but does not include any part of the Commonwealth of Puerto Rico.” The Eastern–Central boundary in **49 CFR 71.5(a)** (Minnesota-Michigan-Wisconsin) runs through the western Upper Peninsula along the lines of Gogebic, Ontonagon, Iron, Dickinson, and Menominee Counties, then follows the western boundary of Michigan through Lake Michigan to the Michigan–Indiana line. The four western Upper Peninsula counties of Gogebic, Iron, Dickinson, and Menominee lie west of that line and are in the central zone under **49 CFR 71.6**. The remainder of Michigan, including the entire Lower Peninsula, is east of the line and is in the eastern zone. **49 CFR 71.5(h)** places municipalities located on the zone boundary itself in the central zone; none of the five cities sits on that boundary. Detroit (Wayne County), Grand Rapids (Kent County), Warren (Macomb County), Sterling Heights (Macomb County), and Ann Arbor (Washtenaw County) are all in the Lower Peninsula, well east of the Upper Peninsula boundary. Therefore all five are labeled **Eastern Time**.
